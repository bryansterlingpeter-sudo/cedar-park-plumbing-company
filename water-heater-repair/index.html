Water Heater Repair in Cedar Park, TX

Water heater repairs for no hot water, leaks, rumbling tanks, and faulty valves.

Call (512) 986-2512 Ask a question

Water reaching the hallway from a heater is not the same job as a tank that has stopped making hot water. A puddle at the utility-room threshold points to a leak path. Cold water at every fixture points more often to a burner, element, thermostat, or control fault.

We trace the wet area, check the tank connections, and test the heating components. We handle both a leaking water heater and a heater that is running without producing hot water.

An active leak, gas smell, or water near electrical connections should not wait. A heater with no hot water but no leak may have less immediate risk, but a failing valve or corroded tank can damage the surrounding floor if left in service.

No hot water or rumbling

People call us for no hot water at the shower, a pilot light that will not stay lit, popping or rumbling from the tank, and water dripping from the relief discharge pipe. Other calls involve rusty hot water, a breaker that trips after the heater starts, or a burner that cycles off before the tank heats.

A rumble can come from sediment at the tank bottom. A relief pipe can drip because of a valve, excess water pressure, or overheating. These faults can sound alike from outside the closet, but the part involved and the repair work are not the same.

Tank and burner callout

At the callout, we read the rating plate, inspect the tank jacket and connections, and check the shutoff valve. We test the power supply on electric units or the burner, pilot, vent, and gas controls on gas units. We also measure water pressure and check hot-water temperature at a nearby faucet.

A basic diagnostic check commonly takes thirty to sixty minutes, depending on access and the fault. Elements, thermostats, relief valves, and accessible fittings can often be replaced during the same visit if the needed part is available. A cracked tank, a seized isolation valve, or a discontinued control may require a separate visit for the ordered part or replacement unit.

Tank age, valves, access

Tank age changes the scope of a repair. Corrosion around the drain valve, fittings, or tank seam can make a small leak different from a replaceable valve problem. An older model may also use a control, element, or burner part that must be ordered rather than fitted from common stock.

Access matters as well. A heater in an open garage is simpler to reach than a unit in a tight closet with a finished wall, platform, or limited drain path. A seized shutoff valve can add work before the heater can be isolated, and local permit or inspection sign-off may apply if the job moves from repair to tank replacement.

Model plate and access

Find the model number on the rating plate attached to the tank jacket if it is readable. Note the installation age, or the serial number if the date is not known. Tell us which room holds the heater and whether water is running from the tank, a valve, or the relief pipe. Let us know if the unit sits in a closet, attic, garage, or other restricted space. Call Cedar Park Plumbing Company at (512) 986-2512, especially if water is pooling, because a tank leak can spread into flooring and framing.
